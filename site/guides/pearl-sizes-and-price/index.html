Pearl sizes and price: how millimetres change what you pay

Tianqi Jewelry · Shanxiahu, Zhuji · Updated 13 November 2026

Size is the easiest pearl factor to specify and one of the biggest drivers of price. This guide explains how pearls are measured, the size ranges of each pearl type, how many pearls go into a strand, and how to balance size against quality within a budget.

How pearl size is measured

Pearl size is the diameter in millimetres. Round and near-round pearls are measured straight across; drop, oval, button and baroque pearls are usually given as length × width.

Wholesale lots are quoted in 0.5 mm ranges, such as 7.0–7.5 mm: every pearl in the lot falls inside that range. A range of 7–8 mm is wider and cheaper to fill, but the pearls will look less even side by side. If you sell pairs or uniform strands, ask for a 0.5 mm range and put matching in your spec.

When you check photos, ask for a caliper reading on a few pearls rather than a ruler next to the lot.

Each type is priced on its own scale. A 9 mm freshwater pearl and a 9 mm Akoya pearl are different products, so compare sizes within a type.

Why price rises faster than size

A larger pearl spends longer growing, and the longer it grows the more chances there are for marks and uneven shape. Fewer pearls reach the larger sizes with good lustre and a clean surface, so each extra half-millimetre costs more than the one before, and the steps get steeper near the top of each type's range.

Size also multiplies through a design. A strand of larger pearls needs fewer pearls, but each one costs more, and matching them takes a bigger pool to choose from.

Strands: sizes, lengths and pearl counts

Strands are either uniform (all pearls in one 0.5 mm range) or graduated (larger in the centre, written end-to-centre, such as 6–9 mm). Common necklace lengths are about 16 in (40 cm, choker) and 18 in (45 cm, princess); longer matinee and opera lengths are made to order.

Choosing sizes by product

  • Stud earrings: many everyday lines use 6–8 mm; smaller sizes suit minimalist or children's styles, larger ones statement pieces. Order matched pairs, half-drilled.
  • Drop earrings and pendants: drop, button and baroque shapes are given as length × width; a single pendant pearl can go larger than a strand for the same budget.
  • Necklaces: choose a uniform or graduated strand and a length, then count pearls with the table above.
  • Bracelets: usually smaller than the matching necklace, so the piece sits comfortably on the wrist.
  • Beading and DIY: small freshwater pearls (2–5 mm) and keshi are popular for layered and handmade designs.

Size or quality? Working to a budget

For a fixed price per piece, you usually trade size against lustre and surface. Stepping down half a millimetre often frees enough budget for noticeably better lustre, which end customers see first. For pieces sold on a "bigger is better" story, the opposite trade may sell better.

The quickest way to decide is to compare two samples side by side: the size you want at a lower grade, and half a millimetre smaller at a higher grade. Tell us your target price and quantity and we will quote both with real photos.

Questions buyers ask

Why does a 9 mm pearl cost so much more than an 8 mm pearl?

Larger pearls need longer in the mollusc, and fewer of them grow clean and well shaped. So price rises faster than size, and the steps get steeper near the top of each pearl type's size range.

How are non-round pearls measured?

Round and near-round pearls are measured across the diameter. Drop, oval, button and baroque pearls are usually given as length × width in millimetres.

How many pearls are in a 16-inch strand?

Divide the length by the pearl size: about 406 mm ÷ 7 mm ≈ 58 pearls for a 7–7.5 mm strand. Knots between pearls and the clasp reduce the count slightly.

What size should I choose for stud earrings?

It depends on your customers and price point. Many retail lines use 6–8 mm for everyday studs and go larger for statement pieces. Tell us your target price and we will suggest sizes and grades that fit.

Do you sell by piece, by strand or by weight?

Our catalogue lists items per piece, pair or strand, with a minimum of 5 pcs per item and USD 100 per order. Prices depend on grade, size and quantity, and we quote each request with real photos.
